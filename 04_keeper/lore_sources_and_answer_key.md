# Lore sources and answer key (instructor only; do NOT paste into the model prompt)

Every claim in the keeper's lore was taken from the pages below. Folklore varies by region
and by teller, so these are "the commonly told version in these sources", not ground truth.

## In the keeper's lore (8)
| Ghost | What the lore says (summary) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Krasue (กระสือ) | Floating woman's head, viscera hanging, glow, feeds on blood/raw flesh/livestock at night, must rejoin body before dawn; thorns/thorny fences; don't dry laundry outside overnight | Wikipedia "Krasue" |
| Krahang (กระหัง) | Male spirit, often told alongside Krasue; flies at night using two kradong (winnowing baskets) or rides a rice-pestle; may harm people walking in remote areas | Wikipedia "Krahang" |
| Phi Pop (ผีปอบ) | Eats raw meat, possesses people/animals and devours internal organs; linked to witchcraft; strongest in the Northeast; exorcism by spirit doctors/monks | Wikipedia "Ghosts in Thai culture"; Thaiger |
| Nang Tani (นางตานี) | Young woman in green Thai dress in wild-banana (kluai tani) clumps; most visible at full moon; mostly gentle but may harm men who wrong women; cloth tied round trunk, offerings of sweets/incense/flowers; don't cut the clump | Wikipedia "Nang Tani" |
| Phi Phong (ผีโพง) | Lanna/Isan; looks ordinary by day; small flame in nose and waxy drip at night; eats filth; throws things on roofs when angry, bringing illness/misfortune | Wikipedia "Phong (ghost)" |
| Kong Koi (ผีกองกอย) | Deep-forest ghost of the Northeast/west Laos; one leg, hops, cries "koi koi koi"; sucks blood from sleepers' toes; can't climb trees; sleep with feet together/crossed, chant | Wikipedia "Kong koi" |
| Phi Tai Hong (ผีตายโหง) | Ghost of someone who died suddenly/violently (accident, murder); restless, haunts the place; offerings to appease | Wikipedia "Ghosts in Thai culture"; Thaiger |
| Pret (เปรต) | Buddhist hungry ghost; very tall, emaciated, needle-eye mouth, always hungry; shown in temple murals as a karma warning; sins such as greed or disobeying parents | Wikipedia "Ghosts in Thai culture"; Thaiger |

Notes where sources are soft or differ:
- Krasue by day: sources only say the body is hidden and must be rejoined by dawn; they do not all agree on what she looks like in daytime. The lore avoids claiming more.
- Krahang: Wikipedia says only that he haunts the same areas as Krasue; "paired with Krasue" is how storytellers and Thaiger present him. The lore does not call him her husband.
- Phi Phong is also called Phi Pao; the lore keeps only the commonly listed traits.

## Deliberately LEFT OUT (use to demonstrate hallucination)
Ask these. A guarded prompt (A) should say it does not know. The unguarded prompt (B) will
probably invent details. Compare with the facts below.

1. **Mae Nak Phra Khanong (แม่นาก)** - Wikipedia "Mae Nak Phra Khanong": legend set at Phra Khanong canal, Bangkok (reign of Rama IV in local lore); Nak died in childbirth while husband Mak was away at war; Mak found her and the child waiting; she stretched her arm to pick up a dropped lime and he realised she was a ghost; he hid behind a Blumea bush / ran into Wat Mahabut; a monk trapped her spirit in an earthen jar thrown into the canal (variants differ; in one, Somdet To binds her spirit to his waistband). Shrine at Wat Mahabut, moved in 1997. Earliest written account 10 March 1899 (K.S.R. Kulap, Siam Prabheth), where the husband is "Chum".
2. **Nang Takhian (นางตะเคียน)** - Wikipedia "Ghosts in Thai culture": tree spirit living in Hopea odorata (ton takhian). Little more is documented in these sources, so any rich detail the model gives (e.g. a specific origin story) is unverified.
3. **Suea Saming (เสือสมิง)** - Wikipedia "Ghosts in Thai culture": a person who becomes a tiger through black magic, similar to a skin-walker; Thaiger ties it to Northern Thai forests.
